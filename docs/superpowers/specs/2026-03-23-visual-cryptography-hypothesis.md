### 3.2 Why "15% Red"?

The Kinko's order specifies two parameters:

**15% reduction:** The transparency was printed at 15% of original size (or reduced BY 15% to 85% scale). This means it was **scaled to match a specific target document.** Candidates:
- The encoding chart (31 columns × 14 rows) reduced to match the sculpture's physical dimensions
- A photograph of the sculpture panel reduced to match the chart
- The 7×14 K4 grid section of the 31-column panel

**Red color:** Red has specific properties relevant to visual cryptography:
- **Darkroom safe light:** Photographic darkrooms use red light because it doesn't expose light-sensitive paper. A red transparency viewed under red light would become invisible, revealing only non-red content beneath it.
- **Color separation:** Red transparency overlaid on a document printed in red ink makes the red content disappear (same color = invisible). Content in other colors shows through. This is how "magic decoder" children's toys work — and it's a legitimate visual cryptography technique.
- **Photographic filter:** A red filter in contact printing selectively exposes certain areas based on the negative's density at red wavelengths.

### 3.3 Why Old Town Alexandria?

Old Town Alexandria, Virginia is:
- ~10 miles from CIA headquarters in Langley
- A historic district with a regular street grid
- The kind of document nobody would find suspicious in an artist's papers

In visual cryptography, **Share B should look innocuous**. A tourist map of a nearby historic district is perfect cover. The actual information is encoded in which cells of the grid are opaque vs transparent — the "map" content is irrelevant; the pattern of dark/light areas IS the message.

**Alternative:** The Old Town Alexandria print may not be Share B at all. It could be:
