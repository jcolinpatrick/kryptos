## Overview

This document formalizes the constraints that any K4 decipherment mechanism must satisfy, derived from two independent sources: (1) Bean's 2021 cryptodiagnostic analysis of the ciphertext and known plaintext, and (2) our confirmed stego layer properties. The novel contribution is the **coupling constraints** — properties where the stego and cipher layers share structure, explaining Bean's statistical anomalies through the Polybius grid that generates the null palette.

These constraints are organized into four tiers, from strongest to weakest.

## Tier 1 — Coupling Constraints (PRIMARY — novel contribution)

These derive from the intersection of confirmed stego properties and cipher layer properties. They explain WHY Bean's statistical anomalies exist.

### C×S-1: Keystream palette enrichment

- **Observation:** 13/24 keystream values are palette letters {B,G,I,K,O,W,Z}
- **Expected under random:** 6.46 (24 × 7/26)
- **p-value:** 0.0043 (binomial tail)
- **Joint significance with palette restriction:** p = 1.4e-7 (MC 50M, proper joint simulation)
- **Implication:** The key generator preferentially produces values from the 7-letter palette. Since this palette is generated by KRYPTOS×SEVEN on a 5-wide KA Polybius grid, the key generation process shares structure with the stego layer.

### C×S-2: Mod-5 KA structure (explains Bean's mod-5 finding)

- **Observation:** 14/24 keystream values have KA_index mod 5 ∈ {0, 3}
- **Expected under random:** 10.15 (24 × 11/26; 11 of 26 letters qualify)
- **p-value:** 0.084 (binomial tail — weaker than C×S-1)
- **Relationship to C×S-1:** Palette membership implies mod-5 satisfaction (all 7 palette letters occupy KA Polybius columns 0 and 3). C×S-2 is strictly weaker than C×S-1 but provides the STRUCTURAL EXPLANATION: the palette enrichment arises because keystream values cluster in specific columns of the 5-wide KA Polybius grid.
- **Explains Bean's finding:** Bean (2021) reported mod-5 structure in the reversed-KA keystream (13/24, p≈1/1,470). Our stego analysis reveals this is because the Polybius grid that generates the null palette also constrains the keystream.

### C×S-3: Arithmetic progression contained in palette

- **Observation:** The values G(6), K(10), O(14) form a step-4 arithmetic progression in the standard (A=0) alphabet. All three are palette letters. These appear at 12/24 keystream positions (50%).
- **Expected under random:** 2.77 (24 × 3/26)
- **p-value:** 3.9e-6 (binomial tail)
- **Dual-alphabet property:** The AP is arithmetic in the STANDARD alphabet (step 4) but NOT arithmetic in the KA alphabet (KA positions: K=0, O=5, G=13; gaps are 5 and 8). This means the mechanism has regularity in AZ and structure in KA — both alphabets participate.

### C×S-4: Dual-alphabet structure

- **Observation:** The stego layer operates in KA space (5-wide KA Polybius grid). The cipher layer has step-4 regularity in standard A=0 space. Both layers couple through the palette.
- **Implication:** The cipher mechanism involves BOTH the standard A-Z alphabet (for arithmetic operations) and the KRYPTOS-keyed alphabet (for structural organization). This is consistent with a Quagmire-type tableau or a cipher that maps between AZ and KA.

## Tier 2 — Bean Statistical Constraints (supporting evidence)

| ID | Constraint | K4 Value | Threshold | Source |
|----|-----------|----------|-----------|--------|
| SC-4 | Minor differences sum (KRYPTOS-set PT letters) | 20 | ≤ 21 | Bean 2021 Table 2, p=1/5,520 |
| SC-5 | Same-PT clustering mean distance | 3.615 | ≤ 3.7 | Bean 2021 Table 3, p=1/240 |

**SC-4 computation:** For each plaintext letter in {K,R,Y,P,T,O,S} that appears at multiple crib positions, find the corresponding ciphertext letters and compute the shortest circular distance between each pair in the standard alphabet. Sum all distances.

**SC-5 computation:** For ALL repeated plaintext letters (not just the KRYPTOS set), compute shortest circular distances between all pairs of corresponding ciphertext letters. Take the mean.

**Interpretation (Bean 2021):** These properties strongly indicate one-to-one letter substitution without transposition. The ciphertext letters for repeated plaintext letters are unusually close in the alphabet, consistent with a cipher alphabet that is "near" the standard alphabet (e.g., keyword-mixed).

## Mechanism Compliance Checklist

Use this to evaluate any proposed K4 solution:

### Step 1: Hard Gates (all must pass)
- [ ] Does the mechanism produce keystream `JLJODEGKUKKKLOCGGBGOKTRU` at the 24 crib positions under Beaufort A=0?
- [ ] Does k[27] = k[65]?
- [ ] Do all 242 Bean inequality pairs have distinct key values?
- [ ] Is the mechanism non-periodic?

### Step 2: Coupling Properties (primary scoring)
- [ ] Do ≥13/24 keystream values fall in {B,G,I,K,O,W,Z}?
- [ ] Do ≥12/24 keystream values fall in {G,K,O} (step-4 AP)?
- [ ] Does the mechanism involve both standard (AZ) and KRYPTOS-keyed (KA) alphabets?

### Step 3: Supporting Evidence
- [ ] Is the minor differences sum ≤ 21?
- [ ] Is the same-PT clustering mean ≤ 3.7?
- [ ] Does the key generation have a mod-5 component?
- [ ] Is the mechanism hand-executable?
- [ ] Is the key derived from a 5-wide grid?

### Scoring
- Any Step 1 failure → **ELIMINATED**
- Step 1 all pass + ≥3 Step 2 checks → **COMPLIANT**
- Step 1 all pass + <3 Step 2 checks → **PARTIAL** (investigate further)
