## 1. Corpus Map

### 1.1 Primary Training Pair: Typed Maintenance Instructions vs. Handwritten Yellow-Pad Drafts

The typed pages (pp. 23–29) contain the finished "Maintenance Instructions for The C.I.A. Kryptos Project, Submitted by Jim Sanborn, 1990" — 5 pages of typewritten text covering materials maintenance, pool maintenance, and landscape maintenance.

The handwritten pages (pp. 33–47) are yellow legal-pad drafts of that same material. Sanborn numbered them in the upper-right corner (circled numbers 2–9). **Page "1" of the handwritten draft is missing from the PDF** — it would have contained the title, introductory paragraph, and the start of the "General Materials Maintenance" section.

### 1.3 Authorship Attribution Notes

- **Yellow-pad pages (pp. 33–47):** Attributed to Sanborn with high confidence. Content directly parallels the typed document "Submitted by Jim Sanborn, 1990." Handwriting is consistent across all pages. These are clearly the working drafts from which the typed version was produced.
- **Page 11 (granite order):** Signed "Jim Sanborn" with address matching the typed document. Red/brown pen, printed style. Confirmed Sanborn.
- **Pages 53–65 (landscape drawings):** All-caps block lettering consistent with Sanborn's architectural notation style but could also be from collaborator James Urban. Attribution uncertain; labeled "probably Sanborn or Urban."
- **Page 67 marginalia:** The words in Sanborn's hand appear on a fax sent TO him, so the marginal notes ("1618 Sterling St," "Gentle / Sentinel / BS Central") are Sanborn's. The body note ("Jim: Forwarding...") is from Anya Guilsher.
- **Page 3 annotations:** Circled numbers and "Kind Regards" on the typed letter are Sanborn's editorial marks.

**Typed Page 23 (Page 1 of Maintenance Instructions):**
```
Maintenance Instructions for
The C.I.A. Kryptos Project

Submitted by Jim Sanborn, 1990

This project for the Agency is a site-specific artwork which
means that the design would not necessarily be suitable on any
other site and that it was designed specifically for this
location.

The way that this artwork looks at completion is the way it
should look for many years. Maintenance then on this piece, and
most importantly on the landscape immediately adjacent to the
piece, should be controlled very carefully in order for the work
to continue looking as it does today.

1. GENERAL MATERIALS MAINTENANCE

Granite, slate, petrified wood and copper are extremely
durable materials. There are however, certain chemicals and
practices which have to be avoided adjacent to the artwork in
order to extend the already long life of these materials.

1a. Accelerated Chemical Weathering

Rock salt and Calcium Carbonate for ice control may stain
and increase the weathering of the copper, granite and
petrified wood. Lawn sprays and fertilizer may stain or
discolor the materials.

It is very important that the contractors in charge of
maintaining the landscape and plaza areas be given copies of
these instructions, as well as the individuals who will be
responsible for the stewardship and conservation of the
artwork at the agency.

1b. Stain or Graffiti Removal

Generally the least evasive method should be attempted
first. The methods are listed below with number 1 being
attempted first and number 5 used as a last resort. All
cleaning should be done by a qualified restoration contractor
or by a representative of the artist.

1. High pressure water blasting or hand brushing with
   clean water, only low pressure on the petrified tree.
2. High pressure water blasting or hand brushing with
   detergent and clean water, only low pressure on the
   petrified tree.
3. Chemical solvents matched to the materials being
   removed.
4. High pressure water blasting with mild abrasives,
   i.e. walnut shell, corn cob, glass beads, low pressure
```

## 3. Sanborn Handwriting Profile

### 3.1 General Characteristics

**Writing style:** Right-slanting cursive with frequent print-cursive mixing. Lowercase letters are generally connected within words but with frequent pen lifts mid-word. Writing becomes more compressed and less careful toward the bottom of pages.

**Slant:** Consistent rightward slant of approximately 15–20° from vertical.

**Baseline:** Generally follows the yellow-pad ruled lines well on the draft pages. On unruled white paper (pp. 11, 19, 55), baseline drifts slightly downward to the right.

**Size:** Medium — approximately 3–4mm x-height on ruled paper. Ascenders (b, d, h, k, l, t) extend about 2x the x-height. Descenders (g, p, y) drop about 1.5x below baseline.

**Pressure:** Medium-heavy, consistent. No significant pressure variation within words.

**Speed:** Moderate to fast. Evidence of speed: connected letter groups, simplified letter forms, occasional omission of dots (i) and crosses (t).

### 3.6 Spacing and Layout Habits

- **Left margin:** Consistent ~1 inch from left edge on yellow pad pages
- **Indentation:** Sub-sections indented approximately 0.5 inches
- **Line spacing:** Generally uses every ruled line, but leaves blank lines between sections/paragraphs
- **Page numbering:** Circled number in upper-right corner, starting at 2
- **Section numbering:** Uses a hierarchical system (1, 1a, 1b, 2, 2A, 2b, etc.) but inconsistently — sometimes uses circled numbers for sub-lists
- **Word spacing:** Moderate and consistent — words are clearly separated
- **Line-end behavior:** Words are not hyphenated at line ends; Sanborn breaks at word boundaries

### 3.7 Correction and Revision Habits

From the 9 pages of handwritten draft, the following correction patterns are documented:

1. **Single-line strikethrough:** The most common correction method. A single horizontal line through the rejected text, with the replacement written immediately after. Examples:
   - ~~color~~ → "original" (p. 35, line 9)
   - ~~the panel~~ → "you will find enclosed" (p. 37, line 17)
   - ~~then~~ → deleted (p. 43, line 25)
   - ~~recommendations~~ → deleted (p. 43, line 11)
   - ~~any~~ → deleted but restored in typed version (p. 45, line 17)

2. **Marginal insertion:** When a strikethrough requires a word to be relocated, Sanborn writes it in the left margin. Example:
   - "color" written in left margin (p. 35) to fix word order

3. **False starts:** Occasionally begins a word or phrase, then strikes through and restarts. Example:
   - ~~re pools~~ (p. 45, line 25) — started writing "re pools" then abandoned

4. **Renumbering:** Section numbers are sometimes struck through and rewritten:
   - ~~2bc~~ → "3C" (p. 43, line 1)
   - ~~e~~ → removed (p. 41, line 17)

5. **No arrows or carets observed** in the maintenance draft. Corrections are inline.

6. **No marginal notes** of substantial length — only single-word margin insertions.

## 5. Confusion Matrix

### 5.1 High-Risk Glyph Confusions

| Confusion pair | Risk | Resolution strategy | Concrete examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **m** vs **in** | CRITICAL | Count humps: "m" = 3 humps, "in" = 2+2. In fast writing, "m" may have only 2 visible humps. Use word context. | "maintenance" — the "m" could read "in" if you miss the third hump. "materials" — initial "m" sometimes looks like "in". |
| **m** vs **rn** | HIGH | In Sanborn's cursive, "rn" has a sharper transition between the r-uptick and the n-hump. "m" has smoother continuous humps. | "motor" vs hypothetical "rnotor" — check for smooth vs. angular transition. |
| **cl** vs **d** | HIGH | Sanborn's "d" has a shorter ascender that curves left. "cl" has a tall straight "l" after an open "c". Width: "d" is narrower than "cl". | "cleaning" — initial "cl" could be read as "d" → "deaning". Context resolves. |
| **e** vs **c** | MEDIUM | Sanborn's "e" has a visible closing loop/crossbar. "c" is simply open. When "e" is written fast, the loop may not close. | "copper" — second letter is clearly "o" not "e". "cleaning" — "e" vs "c" at position 3 resolved by context. |
| **r** vs **v** | MEDIUM | Sanborn's "r" has a shorter upstroke, "v" goes to a pointed bottom. When connected to following letters, "r" has a rounded top, "v" has angular bottom. | "very" — initial "v" could look like "r" → "rery". "various" — "v" confirmed by typed parallel. |
| **t** vs **l** | MEDIUM | Look for crossbar. Sanborn crosses his t's about 75% of the time. When uncrossed, context and word shape resolve. | "the" vs "lhe" — never ambiguous in practice due to "th" being the most common English digraph. "water" vs "waler" — crossbar usually visible. |
| **y** vs **g** | MEDIUM | Sanborn's "y" descender goes straight down or slightly right with a small hook. "g" descender makes a counter-clockwise loop back up. | "courtyard" — "y" has straight descender. "grasses" — "g" has looped descender. But in fast writing, both can look similar. |
| **o** vs **a** | MEDIUM | Both are oval forms. "a" has a slightly open top or a rightward exit stroke. "o" is more completely closed. | "contractor" — the "o" and "a" in this word are distinguishable mainly by position and word knowledge. |
| **u** vs **n** | LOW | "u" curves down-up-right. "n" goes up-down-right. Entry stroke direction is the key. | "natural" — "n" at start vs "u" in middle. |
| **1** vs **l** | LOW | In numeric context, "1" is a simple straight line. In word context, "l" is identical but taller and part of a word. | "12"" — "1" in numeric context. "level" — "l" in word context. Never truly ambiguous in this corpus. |
| **2** vs **Z** | LOW | Sanborn's "2" has a more horizontal base. "Z" would have angular strokes. Only relevant in mixed alphanumeric contexts. | "2740" vs hypothetical "Z740" — resolved by context (address). |
| **5** vs **S** | LOW | "5" has a flat top and curved bottom. Capital "S" is a continuous double curve. | "500" vs "S00" — resolved by context. "5pm" clearly numeric. |

## 6. Reusable Transcription Protocol

### 6.1 Pre-Transcription Checklist

Before attempting any Sanborn transcription:

1. **Identify the document type:** Is this a yellow-pad draft, a white-paper note, an architectural drawing, or a marginal annotation? Each has different characteristics.
2. **Check for typed parallels:** Does a typed version of this content exist? If so, use it as the primary reference.
3. **Inventory the page:** Note page numbers, section numbers, struck-through sections, marginal notes, and drawings before reading individual words.
4. **Establish the domain vocabulary:** What subject matter is this? Kryptos maintenance? Landscape design? Budget? Granite ordering? The expected vocabulary constrains word readings.

### 6.2 Reading Priority Order

When deciphering an ambiguous word:

1. **Document context first:** What section is this in? What topic? What would make sense in this instruction?
2. **Typed parallel (if available):** Check the corresponding typed page.
3. **Repeated Sanborn wordforms:** Has this word appeared elsewhere in confirmed form? Check the Confirmed Lexicon (Section 4).
4. **Stroke analysis:** Count humps (m vs. in vs. rn), check ascender height (d vs. cl), look for crossbars (t vs. l), check descender shape (y vs. g).
5. **Domain vocabulary prior:** Prefer words from the domain lexicon over uncommon readings. "maintenance" is always more likely than "naintenance" in this corpus.
6. **NEVER guess:** If none of the above resolves the reading, mark it as uncertain with ranked candidates.

### 6.3 Diplomatic vs. Normalized Transcription

**Diplomatic transcription** preserves:
- Original line breaks
- Original spelling (including errors)
- Strikethroughs, shown as ~~struck text~~
- Marginal insertions, shown as [margin: "text"]
- Circled numbers, shown as ① ② etc.
- Underlining, shown as __underlined text__
- Illegible segments, shown as [?] or [??word??]
- Page numbers and section numbers as written
- Capitalization as written
- Punctuation as written (including missing punctuation)
- Drawings/diagrams noted as [DIAGRAM: description]

**Normalized transcription:**
- Expands abbreviations
- Corrects obvious spelling errors (noted in brackets)
- Standardizes capitalization
- Restores struck-through corrections to their intended final reading
- Joins split words
- Adds standard punctuation
- Notes significant changes from diplomatic version

### 6.4 Confidence Levels

| Level | Code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| HIGH | (H) | Word is clearly legible AND confirmed by typed parallel or repeated Sanborn wordform |
| MEDIUM | (M) | Word is legible but lacks typed parallel, OR has minor ambiguity resolved by context |
| LOW | (L) | Word is partially legible; reading is the best candidate but alternatives exist |
| UNCERTAIN | (?) | Multiple plausible readings; candidates listed |
| ILLEGIBLE | [IL] | Cannot be read with any confidence |

### 6.5 Handling Special Features

- **Strikethroughs:** Always transcribe the struck text. It reveals Sanborn's thought process and may contain important vocabulary.
- **Marginal notes:** Transcribe with position indicator: "[left margin:]", "[right margin:]", "[above line:]"
- **Arrows:** Note direction and what they connect: "[arrow from X to Y]"
- **Circled text:** Note: "[circled: text]"
- **Underlined text:** Note: "__text__"
- **Numbering changes:** Note original and revised numbers: "~~2bc~~ 3C."
- **Drawings:** Describe position, content, labels: "[DIAGRAM: cross-section of pool showing water level marks]"
- **Multiple ink colors:** Note changes: "[in red:]", "[in blue:]"

### 6.6 Anti-Hallucination Rules

1. **Never infer words from partial strokes alone.** If you can't match at least 50% of the letterforms in a word to known Sanborn shapes, mark it uncertain.
2. **Never assume the draft matches the typed version exactly.** Allow for revisions, additions, deletions, and rewordings.
3. **Never silently regularize spelling** in the diplomatic transcription. If Sanborn wrote "fertilizers" (plural), transcribe it, even though the typed version has "fertilizer" (singular).
4. **Never fill in gaps by copying from the typed version.** If a word is illegible in the handwriting, mark it [?], not what the typed version says.
5. **Prefer "I cannot read this" over "this probably says X."**

## 9. Final Assessment: Reliability of KryptosBot's Sanborn Handwriting Reading

### 9.1 Strengths

1. **The training corpus is excellent.** Nine pages of handwritten draft with a direct typed parallel is an unusually strong foundation for handwriting analysis. The word-for-word alignment provides ground truth for hundreds of individual wordforms.

2. **Sanborn's cursive is moderately legible.** His letterforms are consistent, his writing is generally well-spaced, and he writes at a moderate speed that preserves most letter distinctions. This is not a difficult hand to read.

3. **The domain vocabulary is highly constrained.** Kryptos-related documents will use a predictable vocabulary: maintenance, restoration, contractor, copper, patina, pool, landscape, grasses, sculpture, artwork, granite, etc. This dramatically reduces ambiguity.

4. **Sanborn's correction habits are systematic.** Single-line strikethroughs, marginal insertions, and renumbering follow predictable patterns. Corrections can be decoded reliably.

5. **The ALL-CAPS block printing** (landscape drawings) is virtually unambiguous. Any future Sanborn architectural notes should be very easy to read.

### 9.2 Weaknesses

1. **The m/in/rn confusion is real.** In fast writing, Sanborn's "m" can be indistinguishable from "in" or "rn". This must always be resolved by word context, never by letter shape alone.

2. **The training corpus is limited to one document type.** All primary training pairs come from the maintenance instructions. Sanborn's handwriting in other contexts (personal notes, cryptographic work notes, letters) may differ in speed, formality, and care.

3. **No cryptographic vocabulary in the training set.** Words like "cipher", "encrypt", "Vigenère", "alphabet", "key", "plaintext", "tableau" — none of these appear in the maintenance instructions. Reading Sanborn's handwritten cryptographic notes will require extending the model with inference rather than ground truth.

4. **The "a" vs "o" distinction** remains problematic in fast writing and can only be resolved by word context.

5. **Sanborn's writing deteriorates** toward the bottom of pages (smaller, more compressed, less careful). Bottom-of-page readings should be given lower default confidence.

### 9.4 Recommendations for Future Work

1. **Always search for typed parallels first.** Any Sanborn handwritten document may have a typed counterpart in the Archives of American Art collection.

2. **Build the cryptographic vocabulary incrementally.** When reading Sanborn's handwritten cryptographic notes, start with the words you can read confidently and use them as anchors to decode surrounding text.

3. **Use the confirmed lexicon as a lookup table.** Before guessing any word, check whether it appears in the Section 4 lexicon.

4. **Cross-reference with known Kryptos facts.** If a reading produces a recognizable Kryptos term (CT fragment, alphabet name, cipher method), it's much more likely to be correct.

5. **Never read Sanborn's handwriting in isolation.** Always consider: What document is this? What section? What subject? What words would make sense here?
