### 3.3 The Inclinare Piece -- Multiple Stacked Layers of Filtering

> "Inclinare/Aequus: THREE metal sheets stacked vertically on rods. Top sheet has cut words. Light projects through top sheet onto tilted middle sheet, then through mesh square onto level bottom sheet. Multiple layers of projection through stacked perforated sheets."

If the null mask is defined by which letters sunlight passes through at a specific time, then K1's plaintext is literally describing the mechanism: the illusion lies in the nuance of which letters are in light and which are not.

## 9. THE S-CURVE, COPPER, AND PHYSICAL CONSTRUCTION

### 9.1 Physical Form

From the CIA official page:
> "A piece of petrified wood supports a large S-shaped copper screen that looks like a piece of paper coming out of a computer printer."

**Source**: `/home/cpatrick/kryptos/reference/cia_kryptos_page.md`, line 13

### 9.2 Construction Method

From the Smithsonian archive, Sanborn describes the cutting process:

> "3/8 inch thick copper plate"
> "We could cut each letter by hand with jigsaws... or the letters could be cut by a robot using waterjet (50,000 psi water pressure with garnet dust)"
> "We reluctantly began cutting the letters by hand... With two assistants cutting five days a week it took eight months, twenty assistants nine jigsaws and nine hundred blades"
> "Each letter was then drilled through several times so the saw blade could be inserted to cut out the copper inside the scribed lines"

**Source**: `/home/cpatrick/kryptos/reference/smithsonian_archive.md`, lines 318-360

The letters are CUT COMPLETELY THROUGH the copper. They are not engraved or etched -- they are holes in the shape of letters. This is essential for the light projection mechanism: light passes through the letter-shaped holes and projects the letter shapes onto surfaces behind/around the sculpture.

### 9.3 The S-Curve and Viewing Angle

The S-curve is critical because it means:
- Different letters face different directions
- At any given time, sunlight strikes different parts of the screen at different angles
- Some letter cutouts allow light through while others do not, depending on the angle of incidence
- The curvature creates a NATURAL SELECTION of which letters are illuminated

A flat screen would be all-or-nothing: either the sun hits it and ALL letters project, or it doesn't. The S-curve ensures that at any given moment, only SOME letters allow light through -- creating a natural null mask.

### 9.4 The Petrified Wood Backing

> "On the paved surface, supported by a petrified tree, will stand a curved, vertical copper plate. Approximately 2000 letters of the alphabet are cut through this plate."

**Source**: `/home/cpatrick/kryptos/reference/kryptosfan_findings.md`, lines 43-45

> "The tree I chose to support the Kryptos screen was a section of a 100' long specimen lying horizontally, on a precise east-west axis."

**Source**: `/home/cpatrick/kryptos/reference/smithsonian_archive.md`, lines 569-570

The petrified tree was found on a "precise east-west axis" -- the same axis as EASTNORTHEAST. The tree supports the copper screen, positioning it in a specific orientation relative to the cardinal directions. If light projection defines the null mask, the tree's orientation would determine which letters are illuminated at which times.
