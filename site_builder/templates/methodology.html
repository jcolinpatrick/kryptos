Methodology

This page explains how we test theories, score results, and determine whether a cipher approach has been eliminated.

The K4 Ciphertext

K4 consists of 97 characters, and all 26 letters of the English alphabet appear at least once. The ciphertext is shown below with known plaintext positions highlighted:

{{ ct[:21] }}{{ ct[21:34] }}{{ ct[34:63] }}{{ ct[63:74] }}{{ ct[74:] }}

Known plaintext (0-indexed positions, confirmed by Jim Sanborn):

  • Positions 21–33: EASTNORTHEAST (13 characters)
  • Positions 63–73: BERLINCLOCK (11 characters)

These 24 known characters are the foundation of our scoring system. They also produce known keystream values at those positions, which constrain what cipher methods and keys are possible.

Scoring: Crib Matching (0–24)

For any proposed decryption method and key, we check how many of the 24 known plaintext positions produce the correct letter. This gives a score from 0 to 24.

ScoreClassificationMeaning
0–9NOISEExpected performance from a random key/method. Not recorded.
10–17INTERESTINGAbove noise floor. Recorded for completeness, but almost certainly coincidental.
18–23SIGNALStatistically unusual. Warrants investigation, but may be a false positive at high periods.
24FULL MATCHAll 24 cribs match. Requires Bean constraint validation, quadgram analysis, and human review before any claim is made.

Expected Random Scores by Period

A critical subtlety: expected random score depends on the cipher’s period (key length). At higher periods, random keys match more cribs due to underdetermination — there are fewer constraints per key position.

PeriodExpected Random ScoreDiscriminative?

Key insight: Only scores at period ≤ 7 are meaningful discriminators. All high scores at period ≥ 17 are false positives. This is a mathematical property of underdetermination, not a limitation of our testing.

Bean Constraints

In 2021, Richard Bean published additional constraints on the K4 keystream, derived from the repeated letter P at positions 27 and 65 of the ciphertext (both of which decrypt to R). This gives:

  • Equality: k[27] = k[65] (the key values at these positions must be equal)
  • 242 Variant-Independent Inequalities: pairs of key positions that must differ under all three cipher variants (Vigenère, Beaufort, Variant Beaufort). Derived from all C(24,2) = 276 crib pairs, minus 13 equalities and 21 mixed pairs.

These constraints are variant-independent — they hold whether the cipher is Vigenère, Beaufort, or Variant Beaufort. Any valid solution must satisfy all Bean constraints. The full 242-pair inequality set, combined with a fingerprint argument (key value O=14 appears at positions 24, 63, and 69, forcing any period to divide gcd(39,45)=3, but the key has 12 distinct values), proves that no periodic key of any length — period 1 through infinity — can produce the K4 crib decryptions. This is a complete mathematical proof, not a search result.

Two-System Model

Jim Sanborn stated at the Kryptos dedication that “there are two systems of enciphering the bottom text.” Ed Scheidt confirmed a “bit of stego” and that the “masking technique may not be known.” Combined with the mathematical impossibility of periodic substitution at any period, this means K4 uses two layers: a steganographic layer (null mask) and a substitution cipher.

Pure transposition is independently impossible: the ciphertext contains only 2 E’s, but the known cribs require 3. Therefore at least one layer must be substitution. The leading model is: 

  PT (73 chars) → substitution cipher → null insertion (24 chars) → carved text (97 chars)

The stego layer is partially characterized: the null palette (letters used as nulls) is {B,G,I,K,O,W,Z}, which corresponds to columns 0 and 3 of a 5-wide Polybius grid built from the KRYPTOS keyword-mixed alphabet (joint null probability p=1.4×10−7, 50M Monte Carlo trials). 17 of 24 null positions are fixed by consensus; 7 remain uncertain.

The cipher layer remains open. Periodic keys are proven impossible at all periods. Running keys from 60,230 publicly available English texts (Project Gutenberg, 106 billion offset-checks) produced zero signal. The surviving possibilities are: a running key from a non-public source, a bespoke procedural cipher, or a one-time key.

What Has Been Exhaustively Tested

Over 930 experiments spanning 882 billion+ configurations have been run, eliminating:

  • All periodic substitution (Vig/Beau/VBeau × AZ/KA × all periods): mathematical proof
  • All autokey variants (PT/CT × all primers): structural impossibility proof via crib-to-crib feedback
  • All sub × transposition MITM (~1.2B configs): mono + periodic + autokey × 14 transposition families
  • All VIC cipher models (1.32B+ configs): generic, narrative, K2-derived, historical params
  • All digraphic ciphers (Four-Square, Playfair, Two-Square): single-layer ceiling 23/24 (overfitting)
  • All bespoke mechanisms: Gromark (8.74B), Feistel, Gronsfeld, Porta, fractionation, homophonic, Hill
  • Running key from 60K Gutenberg texts (106B offset-checks): zero signal
  • All transposition within CT73 + periodic/running key: 4.5M transpositions × 3 variants
  • Sculpture reading paths as key: 10,777 paths × 976K tests, max 7/24 = noise
  • Grid-position-dependent keying: key=f(row,col,keyword), zero signal
  • Morse code hypotheses: Morse Indexed Alphabet + CT→Morse→Binary null mask, all noise

73-Character Hypothesis

Sanborn’s yellow legal pad (auctioned in 2025) shows “8 lines 73” for K4. The carved text has 97 characters — so 24 characters may be nulls, reinforced by the Quadruple-24 Coincidence:

  1. 97 − 73 = 24 nulls
  2. EASTNORTHEAST (13) + BERLINCLOCK (11) = 24 crib characters
  3. The Weltzeituhr (Berlin Clock) has exactly 24 facets
  4. K3’s encoding chart has exactly 24 rows (verified from worksheets)

The Null Palette

Statistical analysis has identified a null palette — a set of 7 letters {B, G, I, K, O, W, Z} that appear disproportionately at null positions (p=6.7×10−5, Monte Carlo 50M trials). These 7 letters correspond exactly to columns 0 and 3 of a 5-wide Polybius grid built from the KRYPTOS keyword-mixed alphabet. This structure was independently confirmed by Richard Bean’s 2021 finding that 13/24 keystream values are multiples of 5.

The joint significance of the palette existing AND producing 13/24 keystream enrichment at crib positions is p=1.4×10−7 (50M trials, proper joint null model accounting for negative correlation between events). This is the strongest confirmed statistical finding in the K4 analysis.

What Remains

Null mask + periodic substitution is proven impossible for ANY choice of 24 null positions at ANY period (algebraic proof extended to CT73 via Bean inequalities across all 11,440 candidate masks). The cipher operating on the 73 extracted characters must use a non-periodic key — a running key from an unknown source, a bespoke procedural method, or a one-time key.

Validation Criteria

A candidate solution is not accepted unless it passes all of the following simultaneously:

  • Crib score: 24/24
  • Bean constraints: PASS
  • Quadgram log-probability: > −4.8 per character (English-like text quality)
  • Index of coincidence consistent with English (IC ≈ 0.067 expected)
  • Semantic coherence: reads as meaningful English with recognizable words (human review required)

Truth Taxonomy

Every claim in our database is classified by its evidence level:

[PUBLIC FACT]
Verified by reputable public reporting or primary-source statements.
[DERIVED FACT]
Deterministic consequence of public facts, reproducible by a provided command.
[INTERNAL RESULT]
Empirical result from this project. Includes artifact pointers and a reproduction command.
[HYPOTHESIS]
Plausible claim not yet proven. Includes a test plan.

Confidence Tiers

Tier 1 — Mathematical Proof (~99.9%)
Algebraic proof that the method cannot produce the known plaintext. Permanently valid unless crib positions or ciphertext transcription are wrong.
Tier 2 — Exhaustive Search (~95%)
Every possible configuration was tested under stated assumptions. Solid for the specific model tested, but does not eliminate multi-layer variants.
Tier 3 — Partial/Statistical (~40–70%)
Sampling-based or incomplete coverage. May warrant re-testing under different assumptions.
Tier 4 — Untested (~0%)
Never properly tested. Fully open.

Reproducibility

Every elimination includes a reproduction command you can run yourself. The entire codebase is open source. Clone the repo, install Python 3.11+, and run any experiment with PYTHONPATH=src.
