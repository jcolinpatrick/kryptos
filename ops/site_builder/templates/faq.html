Frequently Asked Questions

Is Kryptos K4 solved?

No. As of 2026, K4 remains unsolved after 35 years. In 2025 the full plaintext was found in Smithsonian archives but is sealed until 2075. The encryption method remains unknown to the public.

What has already been tried?

Over {{ total_experiments }} experiments covering {{ total_configs_disproven }}+ configurations have been run, testing every major classical cipher family: Vigenère, Beaufort, columnar transposition, Playfair, Bifid, running keys, and more. All single-layer classical ciphers have been eliminated. Browse the full database to see what has been tested.

What approaches remain open?

All standard cipher families have been exhaustively eliminated, including periodic substitution (complete mathematical proof at all periods), all autokey variants (structural impossibility proof), running keys from 60,000+ publicly available English texts (106 billion offset-checks), and every bespoke mechanism tested (Gromark, VIC, Feistel, digraphic, etc.). The main surviving possibilities are: (1) a running key from a non-public source text; (2) a bespoke procedural cipher using Sanborn’s encoding charts (the original K4 chart was sold at auction in 2025 for $962,500); (3) a genuinely novel mechanism (Sanborn has said K4 is “embarrassingly simple once seen.”) See the Research Questions page for the full list of open problems.

Can I submit my own theory?

Yes. Use the Submit a Theory page. Describe your idea in plain English and the theory classifier will check whether it matches anything in the elimination database. If your theory is genuinely novel, it will be queued for evaluation.

If your submission leads to the solution of Kryptos K4, you will receive full credit for the discovery. This site exists solely to support the K4 community.
How do I know your results are correct?

Every elimination includes a reproduction command you can run yourself. The codebase is fully open source. We also classify results by confidence tier and list explicit scope limitations for each elimination. If you find an error, report it and we will investigate.

Who built this?

internal.com is built by Colin Patrick (human lead) and Claude (AI computational partner, by Anthropic). The project began as a systematic attempt to solve K4 using computational cryptanalysis and evolved into a public elimination database so the community can build on our work rather than re-testing approaches already proven fruitless. This site does not know the solution and is not affiliated with the official Kryptos Keepers or their verification site.

Why can’t computers just solve K4?

K4 has only 97 characters, too short for most statistical attacks to distinguish signal from noise. Exhaustive testing has eliminated all standard periodic keys. Sanborn confirmed two layers of encryption, and pure transposition is independently impossible (the ciphertext has 2 E’s but the cribs require 3). The exact two-layer structure is unknown. The information-theoretic deficit is estimated at roughly 138 bits, meaning on the order of 2138 configurations could satisfy the known constraints. (This is an order-of-magnitude estimate that depends on assumptions about plaintext entropy and cipher structure; it should not be treated as a precise bound.) Solving K4 requires identifying the specific method, not just trying all keys.
