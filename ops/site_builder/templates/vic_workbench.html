What is the VIC cipher?

The VIC cipher was used by Soviet spy Reino Häyhänen (code name VICTOR) during the Cold War. It is a sophisticated hand cipher that combines a straddling checkerboard with columnar and disrupted diagonal transpositions, all keyed from a single set of inputs (phrase, date, personal number, keygroup).

Why test it on K4? Ed Scheidt (cryptographic consultant for Kryptos) worked at CIA and NSA where the VIC cipher was studied extensively after its discovery in 1953. Scheidt confirmed K4 uses “two systems of enciphering” — VIC is a real-world precedent for combining substitution (checkerboard) with multiple transpositions in a single hand-executable system.

K2 connection: The VIC cipher uses a straddling checkerboard with row labels derived from key generation. K2’s coordinate numbers (38, 57, 6.5, 44) encode K3 grid dimensions — they may also encode VIC-style checkerboard parameters.
